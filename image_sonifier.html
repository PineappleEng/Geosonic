Image Sonifier

Scans a picture from left to right, or a video through time, and lets it compose. Its shape sets the meter, its colors choose the key and swing, its light chooses the mode, and the detail in each slice shapes how the melody and the air move. It plays as peaceful ambient music: a breathing pad and drone, a sparse melody and arpeggio with drifting echoes, a gliding harmony, sparkles and a soft texture, all in a vast, dark reverb. Pick the instrument for each part in the mixer. Drop in your own image or video (WebM, MP4) or play the sample.

How the image becomes sound

  1. Read the pixels–
  2. MeasureRed, green and blue become brightness maps. From them come light, hue, colorfulness, detail (how much light varies inside a slice) and where the light sits vertically.
  3. Cut into slices, bars and sections–
  4. Make musical choicesEach section takes a key from its hue (walking the circle of fifths) and a mode from its light, Lydian when bright down to gentle minor (Aeolian) when dark. Grey sections use a pentatonic melody. Green picks a chord every two bars, and the pad and bass hold through it. A drone sits on each section's root and fifth. Detail sets the air texture (still, breeze, drift or gust) and variation in blue sets the harp rhythm. Sections end with a soft swell.
  5. Write a smooth melodyRed sets the pitch, averaged across neighbouring slices so texture doesn't make it jitter. Detail, measured against the image's own range, sets the rhythm: most notes are held long, with eighths only in the image's busiest spots. Melody and arpeggio notes echo back and forth between left and right. Leaps are at most three scale steps and are followed by a step back. Fast notes walk toward the next note or turn around it, melody notes ring into each other, and loudness swells over two-bar phrases. When the key changes the melody stays near its last pitch, and the harmony glides between its notes as one line.
  6. Synthesize and mix–
